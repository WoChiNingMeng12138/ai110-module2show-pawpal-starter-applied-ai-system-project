Dog Care Basics

Dogs need at least one walk per day, ideally split into a morning and evening walk of 20-30 minutes each, to maintain healthy weight and mental stimulation. Puppies under 1 year old need shorter, more frequent walks (10-15 minutes, 3-4 times a day) because their joints are still developing. Senior dogs (8+ years) may need shorter, gentler walks but should still go out daily to maintain mobility.

Dogs should be fed twice a day, roughly 12 hours apart, unless a veterinarian recommends otherwise for a specific health condition. Puppies under 6 months often need three smaller meals a day. Fresh water should be available at all times and checked/refilled at least once daily.

Dental care for dogs is often overlooked. Brushing a dog's teeth 2-3 times per week helps prevent plaque buildup and periodontal disease. Dental chews can supplement but not replace brushing.

Grooming needs vary by breed. Short-haired dogs typically need brushing once a week, while long-haired or double-coated breeds may need brushing daily during shedding season. Bathing should happen every 4-6 weeks unless the dog gets visibly dirty sooner; over-bathing can strip natural oils and irritate skin.

Medication for dogs with chronic conditions (e.g., thyroid, arthritis) should be given at the same time every day to maintain consistent blood levels. Missing doses can reduce effectiveness.

Dogs are social animals and benefit from daily play sessions (10-20 minutes) separate from walks, to reinforce bonding and provide mental enrichment. Puzzle toys and training sessions (5-10 minutes daily) also help reduce problem behaviors caused by boredom.

Cat Care Basics

Cats are largely independent but still need daily engagement. Litter boxes should be scooped at least once daily, and fully changed weekly, to prevent cats from avoiding a dirty box. Multi-cat households should have one litter box per cat plus one extra.

Cats should have food available appropriate to their age: kittens under 6 months typically need 3-4 small meals a day, adult cats do well with 2 meals a day (free feeding is discouraged for indoor cats prone to obesity). Fresh water should be checked daily; many cats prefer running water (fountains) over standing bowls.

Indoor cats need daily play time (10-15 minutes) using wand toys or laser pointers to simulate hunting behavior and prevent obesity and boredom-related destructive behavior. This is especially important for cats described as "indoor only".

Grooming for cats: short-haired cats mostly self-groom but benefit from weekly brushing to reduce hairballs. Long-haired cats need brushing every 1-2 days to prevent mats. Nail trims are typically needed every 2-3 weeks.

Cats over the age of 7 are considered mature/senior and should have more frequent (twice yearly) vet checkups and may need adjusted feeding schedules to prevent weight gain as activity decreases with age.

Litter box cleaning, feeding, and fresh water are the three daily non-negotiables for cat care; missing any of them for multiple days can create health or behavioral issues.

General Pet Care Notes

Any pet with "notes" indicating a medical condition (e.g., "on medication", "recovering from surgery", "senior", "needs monitoring") should have care tasks weighted with higher priority and, where relevant, a fixed preferred_time to ensure consistency (e.g., medication given at the same time daily).

For puppies and kittens (age under 1 year), tasks should generally be more frequent but shorter in duration, and prioritized as required since foundational habits and health routines set the stage for adulthood.

When generating a care plan, always include: a feeding-related task, a hygiene/grooming task appropriate to the species, and an activity/enrichment task (walk for dogs, play session for cats). Frequency should default to "daily" for feeding, exercise, and litter care, and "weekly" for grooming/dental unless the species or age suggests otherwise.
